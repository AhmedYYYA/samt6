# SAMT proposal for Ministry of Defence leadership development

Proposed by Ahmed Younis Yousif Alhammadi | 25 September 2026 | Version 1.0

## Decision requested

I propose that the Ministry of Defence endorse SAMT in principle and appoint an institutional sponsor to develop the delivery design, complete feasibility work and coordinate with proposed hosts, before a separate decision to launch a pilot.

The initiative is proposed by Ahmed Younis Yousif Alhammadi. It seeks MOD adoption and does not represent an approved programme or existing partnerships. In-principle endorsement would not itself create a financial commitment or open real nominations.

The decision package includes proposed programme design, governance, selection, learning, assessment, impact follow-up, a cost model and a risk register, alongside a digital demonstration using fictional data.

## Institutional need and value

The initiative addresses a gap identified by its proposer between the accumulated experience of established leaders and the promise of the next generation, alongside technological, industrial, geopolitical and policy change. Its scale should be validated through interviews and workforce-development evidence during feasibility work.

The intended value is to prepare exceptional military and civilian personnel to understand the institutional ecosystem, make better decisions and work across functions. This includes strategy, operations, people, capabilities, acquisition, finance, industry, support and governance.

OECD guidance and leadership-training research inform the use of practice, feedback and transfer to work. These are design rationales, not evidence that SAMT has delivered impact; that requires a pilot and follow-up measurement. [R1, R2]

## Programme charter and boundaries

Participants are exclusively MOD military and civilian personnel at early and middle career stages, selected for exceptional ability and leadership potential. Each cycle is capped at 20; the final size depends on host capacity, supervision and funding.

The fixed duration is 16 consecutive full-time weeks across the UAE, UK, France and US. The reference allocation is 3, 6, 3 and 4 weeks respectively. Transitions and reviews sit within this period; contact hours and travel days require an approved delivery schedule.

Hosts and tracks listed in the stations appendix remain design anchors, with additions allowed where they serve learning outcomes. Every hosting arrangement, seat allocation and site access requires formal agreement. Twelve months of follow-up remains the proposed baseline.

Participation does not confer rank, promotion or automatic eligibility for command. No accredited academic or professional award is claimed without an explicit agreement with an authorised awarding body.

## Nomination and selection

The process begins with institutional nomination supported by performance evidence, full-time availability and a motivation statement. Eligibility is checked first, followed by a case exercise, group exercise and structured interview, then a multidisciplinary panel review.

The proposed selection weighting is 30% analysis and judgement, 25% learning and systems understanding, 20% team leadership, 15% motivation and self-awareness, and 10% communication. Professional conduct and integrity are a separate condition. Criteria should be calibrated in the pilot before any pass threshold is adopted.

At least two assessors document evidence; the panel reviews divergent cases and declares conflicts of interest. Decisions are reasoned and reviewable, with numerical scoring supporting human judgement. No unapproved age, rank or service-length limits are assumed.

## Applied learning and knowledge transfer

All fellows follow a shared core in MOD ecosystem understanding, with assignments adapted to experience and function. Each week connects an institutional question, a host engagement or applied case, a written output and feedback. Fellows record what they learned, how it may be used and what still needs validation.

Each fellow is paired with an experienced mentor. Project teams mix military and civilian perspectives and experience levels. Structured experience discussions, after-action reviews and intergenerational dialogue preserve tacit knowledge and test its relevance in a new context.

The impact project starts in the UAE and develops across all four stations. Educational examples include improving decision flow, knowledge exchange or a support process. An institutional owner approves the challenge, baseline and permitted data scope.

## Assessment and completion

Progress is assessed across eight competency domains. Each has behavioural evidence and outputs, using a 1–5 scale from basic understanding to independent application and reasoned explanation of alternatives. Proposed weights total 100% and are not an approved standard.

Assessment combines baseline, weekly evidence, host and mentor observations, midpoint review, a decision exercise and final briefing. A change on an ordinal rating scale describes progress; it does not alone prove transfer to work.

The panel recommends completion when essential evidence, the impact project, implementation plan and professional-conduct requirements are met. Gaps and remediation are documented when needed; an aggregate score cannot by itself override a material deficiency.

## Governance and responsibilities

An authorised institutional sponsor would own direction and gate decisions, supported by a steering committee covering development, HR, operations, finance, legal affairs and international cooperation. A programme office would manage delivery, records and scheduling; a sufficiently independent assessment panel would own selection and completion decisions.

Each impact project has a benefits owner in the home organisation. Finance verifies cost; the authorised authority approves commitments; hosts approve their content and assessment. Information protection and digital-hosting responsibilities must be set under MOD requirements before real operation.

## Post-programme impact

The proposed 12-month follow-up includes eight mentoring touchpoints in months 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12. Records cover development objectives, project implementation, return-to-work challenges and line-manager feedback.

Delivery quality is measured through evidence and feedback completeness; learning through before-and-after performance tasks; transfer through documented manager and mentor examples; benefits through project outcomes against an agreed baseline.

Report denominators, sample size and non-response. Readiness improvement or process-time reduction requires a definition and data owner; neither is presented as a promise or achieved result. Where practical, use a suitable comparison group while accounting for differences in roles.

## Cost and funding model

Cycle cost combines fixed setup, per-participant training, travel, accommodation, subsistence, insurance and support, plus an approved contingency. Economic cost also includes release time, cover and opportunity cost even where these are not additional payments.

The website provides a planning calculator with editable illustrative inputs. It assumes 91 days abroad within the 3/6/3/4-week allocation. Actual nights, travel days, fees, taxes and insurance require documented quotations and arrangements. Figures are neither host quotations nor a budget approval request.

Before launch, finance should present three cohort-size cases up to 20 participants, sensitivity to exchange rates, cancellation and delay, and a fully scoped cost estimate. The budget ceiling is set after verification, not inferred from demonstration data.

## Digital ecosystem and transition

This release demonstrates nomination, selection, learning, monitoring and assessment. Records, figures and aliases are fictional. Demo changes are saved only in the user’s browser and can be reset; there are no real accounts, messages or submissions to MOD.

Real operation requires an approved role and permissions model, institutional identity, data hosting, retention and deletion, server-side audit, incident response and support, and integrations selected by MOD. These are transition requirements, not security capabilities established by this demonstration.

Delivery begins with detailed design and host validation, followed by pilot preparation and assessment calibration, the 16-week cycle, then impact follow-up and a scale decision. Actual dates depend on approvals and readiness; no confirmed launch date is claimed.

## Appendix on stations and proposed hosts

### United Arab Emirates
Weeks 1–3

Build a shared understanding of MOD and the capability ecosystem, connecting priorities to people, resources, industry and readiness.

Tawazun Council · EDGE Group · GAL · Technology Innovation Institute (TII)

Ecosystem map, competency baseline and an institutional challenge approved for study.

Includes engagement with relevant MOD functions, subject to formal coordination.

### United Kingdom
Weeks 4–9

Team leadership, judgement under pressure, professional discipline and the relationship between technical capability and readiness.

Royal Military Academy Sandhurst · RAF Cosford · Defence Academy and Cranfield University · Relevant UK MOD programmes and institutions

Leadership observation record, decision exercise and system-readiness review.

A proposed tailored pathway for military and civilian personnel; it does not represent admission to a standard commissioning course or a military qualification.

### France
Weeks 10–12

Partnerships, investment, engineering, integration and knowledge transfer, connecting policy to industrial decisions and acquisition.

EDGE Europe — Paris · Engineering and integration track in Bordeaux

A partnership or acquisition case covering integration, risk and knowledge transfer.

EDGE Europe remains the station anchor. Site and activity access must be agreed with the host.

### United States
Weeks 13–16

Government cooperation, FMS, acquisition, programme management and strategic leadership, culminating in an assessed impact-project briefing.

DSCA and DSCU · Naval Postgraduate School — defence resource management track · Government and security cooperation / FMS track · Acquisition, programme management and contracts track · War colleges and strategic-leadership programmes · Practical attachment with an executive entity or programme office

Executive impact-project brief and a 90-day implementation plan.

US institutions and tracks are proposed, not agreed hosting arrangements. Duration, eligibility and locations must be coordinated to fit the four-week station.

## Appendix on the weekly plan

### 1 MOD as a system

Map the relationship between strategy, operations, resources and governance.

### 2 From need to capability

Trace a fictional requirement through planning, acquisition and support.

### 3 Innovation and baseline

Define the impact challenge and record the baseline and development plan.

### 4 Leadership and service

Analyse a leader’s responsibilities and team behaviour in an institutional situation.

### 5 Teams and trust

Lead a diverse team and receive structured feedback.

### 6 Judgement under uncertainty

Present clear options using incomplete information and explicit assumptions.

### 7 Readiness and technical capability

Connect technical qualification, safety and support to readiness needs.

### 8 Resources and life cycle

Compare alternatives within a resource constraint using life-cycle cost.

### 9 Midpoint review

Review evidence and the development plan; discuss the challenge with the mentor.

### 10 Partnerships and industrial strategy

Identify partner interests and the conditions for a successful institutional relationship.

### 11 Engineering and integration

Analyse system interfaces, dependencies and integration risks.

### 12 Knowledge transfer and governance

Present a partnership case distinguishing learning and knowledge transfer from purchasing a product.

### 13 Government cooperation and FMS

### 14 Acquisition and programme management

Review requirements, schedule, cost, risk and governance.

### 15 Geopolitics, policy and technology

Test how an external change affects institutional and technology choices.

### 16 Impact project and final assessment

Defend the project and implementation plan before a panel and record follow-up objectives.

## Appendix on competencies and evidence

### Strategic judgement (20%)

Interpret geopolitical change and connect choices to national and institutional priorities.

An options brief stating assumptions, risks and decision consequences.

### Institutional and systems understanding (15%)

Understand the connections between operations, people, capabilities, resources and governance.

An ecosystem map tracing a requirement to a readiness outcome.

### People leadership and knowledge transfer (15%)

Build trust, lead diverse teams and learn from the experience of earlier generations.

Multi-source behavioural observations and an individual development plan.

### Capabilities, acquisition and resources (15%)

Balance requirements, cost, life cycle, integration and sustainment.

A decision case comparing capability options and whole-life cost.

### Technology and responsible innovation (10%)

Evaluate emerging technology value, limitations, data needs and integration.

An experiment brief connecting the problem, hypothesis and success criteria.

### Governance, policy and ethics (10%)

Distinguish authorities, obligations and risks, and record decisions with integrity.

A memorandum identifying authority, controls and conflicts of interest.

### Partnerships and international engagement (10%)

Communicate across cultures and understand government and industrial cooperation.

A negotiation brief and stakeholder-interest map.

### Delivery and impact measurement (5%)

Turn learning into an implementable, measurable institutional improvement.

An impact project with an executive owner, baseline and follow-up reviews.
